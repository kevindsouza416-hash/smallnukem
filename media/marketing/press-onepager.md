# SMALL NUKEM — BWRX-300 Startup Simulator

## A playable nuclear reactor startup, built on a real plant model — free in your browser

**What it is.** Small Nukem is a 2D side-view, single-player game for phone and desktop browsers. The player is Fluke Nukem, a rookie operator starting up a BWRX-300 small modular reactor from cold shutdown to critical. An old-school chief engineer — "the Chief," appearing in a TV window with his coffee mug — guides the player throughout, and when the player is stuck, demonstrates the task himself on the game's real controls.

**Why it's interesting.** The game is built on a real BWRX-300 plant model — the 300 MWe SMR design from GE Vernova Hitachi that OPG is building at Darlington. Its procedures, physics constants, and safety systems come from that model, so a playthrough is a genuine startup sequence: verify the cold state, follow the procedure chain, bring the reactor critical, synchronize to the grid. Steam vents from joints, flanges, and valves according to the simulator's live pressure and flow. WHMIS safety cards use the exact official pictograms, including the CNSC radiation trefoil. When the player gets it wrong, the plant does what a real plant does: it shuts down safely. There is no explosion ending.

It was made by a father–daughter team: Kevin D'Souza directed and built the game; the BWRX-300 plant model at its core is his daughter Samira's simulator work.

**The game.** Three levels: (1) operator startup, cold to critical; (2) maintenance rounds; (3) a control-room finale with cascading failures. It features photorealistic characters, 23 produced video cinematics with the Chief's narration, and a control-room light-up splash screen. It is one self-contained game that runs in the browser — no download for web play — and is also available as an Android app.
