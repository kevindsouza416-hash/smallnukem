**Date:** 2026-11-26 · **Lane:** Small Nukem controls research (coordinator) · **Status:** research only — no code changed
**Question from Kevin (live call, ~21:09 EST):** "Do some research in terms of games that are similar to this and how it's been handled on all those different input controls. If we have to remap the keys, that's fine."
**Method:** 22 research workers + 2 independent verifiers (24 agents). Worker files with full citations: `workers/` in this folder. Claims are labelled **PROVEN** (a cited source states it) or **SUGGESTED** (inference/transfer). Load-bearing claims were cross-checked: guideline numbers 20/20 CONFIRMED by the verifier against primary sources; game claims 37 CONFIRMED / 2 CONTRADICTED / 5 UNVERIFIABLE out of 44 — the 2 contradictions are corrected in this report and flagged where they occur.

**The game, for reference:** Small Nukem — single-player 2D side-view BWRX-300 plant-operation game, one self-contained HTML file, played primarily in iOS Safari on iPhone, also desktop browsers and Android. The player walks left/right, climbs ladders, stands in front of panels, and operates levers/valves needing precise percentage values, buttons, and a follow/demo assist. Live problems motivating this research: (a) automated input-parity checks keep failing on the harness (synthetic touch timing out), (b) on the Pixel XL pass, on-screen hold-buttons never moved the character under injected holds, (c) the Level 3 emergency (lever cut + bypass raise inside ~12 sim-seconds) proved very hard to drive.

## 2. Findings per research question

### Q1 — Comparable games: how each handles the same actions per input method

- **Movement is hold-based everywhere on touch.** Dead Cells (floating pad), Terraria (left stick), Stardew's joystick schemes, Limbo/INSIDE (hold a side / hold to move), Leo's Fortune/Oddmar (hold/drag) — no shipped comparator uses tap-per-step for continuous walking. Tap-to-move exists only as Stardew's *default option*, and Stardew's own wiki says precision tasks can't be done with it (PROVEN, `g3`, `p1`).
- **Buttons beat a soft joystick for discrete directions.** In the one controlled study found (HCII 2022, n=12), directional Buttons produced 95.5% less out-of-path movement than a soft joystick at p < .005 and were rated most comfortable (PROVEN, small-n, path-following task — `p1`). Dead Cells independently prototyped gesture/combined-button schemes and rejected them for low accuracy, shipping a pad + separate buttons (PROVEN, `g1`).
- **Interact converges on ONE context-sensitive verb.** Limbo's grab drags boxes, pulls levers, presses buttons; Terraria's Smart Interact changes to whatever the cursor is over; Dead Cells mobile added exactly one Interact button. Nobody ships per-object buttons (PROVEN, `g1`, `g2`, `g8`).
- **Climbing folds into the movement axis** (up/down at a ladder) in Limbo, Terraria, and Celeste-style schemes — never a separate climb button (PROVEN, `g2`, `g8`).
- **Precise values are the operation-sim problem, and the shipped answers are: two-stage commit, pause, or remove the precision.** Terraria: Aim Only (position precisely) then a commit button. FTL: pause, then swipe-sliders. Papers, Please phone: delete the precision demand entirely. Nobody ships "drag a small lever precisely with a finger in real time" as the only path (PROVEN, `g2`, `g6`, `g7`).

### Q2 — Touch patterns, target sizes, dwell

- **Target-size numbers (all verifier-CONFIRMED against primary sources):** Apple HIG — hit region **at least 44×44 pt**; games/accessibility tables give 44 pt default, **28×28 pt absolute minimum**; spacing "about" 12 pt padding (bezelled) / 24 pt (unbezelled) (`h1`). Material 3 — touch targets **at least 48×48 dp** (~7–10 mm), ≥8 dp separation; pointer targets 44×44 dp (`h2`). Game Accessibility Guidelines — **2.4 cm ideal, 0.96 cm recommended minimum for phones** (the verifier confirmed GAG *does* publish numbers; an early worker claim that it doesn't was CONTRADICTED and is not used) (`h3`, `v2`). Xbox XAG 107 — suggested minimum defaults **15 mm × 15 mm phones / 24 mm × 24 mm tablets**, player-adjustable size/spacing/position (`h4`). WCAG 2.2 — 2.5.8 minimum **24×24 CSS px** (AA), 2.5.5 enhanced **44×44 CSS px** (AAA) (`h5`).
- **Games go bigger than the minimums, and hit areas exceed the artwork.** Super Crate Box shipped 75×75 pt buttons; Carnage extended hit pads past the largest measured thumb area — when a bug left only the drawn button active, players reported the game as broken (PROVEN, GDC sources in `p1`).
- **Sliding between directions needs ONE movement zone, not separate buttons.** Carnage added slide-without-lifting between directions; a web devlog independently abandoned four separate `<button>` elements for one touch zone with directional sectors (PROVEN as shipped fixes, `p1`). Separate per-direction elements that each need their own touchstart are a known failure shape — directly relevant to live problem (b).
- **Multi-touch must be tracked per pointer.** Each finger needs its own `pointerId`; mouse events are single-pointer only; Phaser enables only one touch pointer by default, so move+action silently fails until more are enabled (PROVEN, `p1`, `t4`).
- **Hold is the norm but guidelines require an alternative.** GAG (Motor, Intermediate): "Avoid / provide alternatives to requiring buttons to be held down" — toggle, automatic, or a player setting. XAG 107 flags holds of **2–3 s or more** and names toggles/auto-holds as the fix (PROVEN, `h3`, `h4`). No study was found measuring fatigue from holding a virtual *walk* button specifically — an honest evidence gap (`p1`).
- **Dwell: the documented pattern is short, visible, and forgiving — Small Nukem's silent 3 s is none of those.** Gaze-dwell norms are **400–1000 ms**; Microsoft's prescribed pattern is a 150–250 ms onset delay (filters glances), then continuous radial-fill progress, completing ~0.8–1.1 s total, and even then dwell is "only a last fall-back" (PROVEN, `p2`, verifier-CONFIRMED). The known failure mode is the **Midas Touch problem** (Jacob 1991): standing-to-read is indistinguishable from intending to activate. **No shipped game was found that operates a panel by dwell alone** — Playdead uses a deliberate press/hold; Limbo's elevator is stand-in-front + tap (PROVEN, `g8`, `p2`). GAG contains no dwell guideline at all; by analogy (hold + precise-timing clauses) a dwell path needs a direct-press alternative and visible progress (SUGGESTED, `h3`).

### Q3 — Keyboard schemes and remapping

- **There is no single platformer default — there are two incompatible families.** Arrows + Z/X/C (Celeste, Hollow Knight) vs WASD + Space/J/K (Shovel Knight PC, Terraria's A/D + Space). One-family-only defaults strand the other family's players (PROVEN per-game, `p5`).
- **Binding both families at once is already shipped practice.** Celeste binds Grab to Z, V, *and* Left Shift simultaneously; Shovel Knight binds Jump to K *and* Space; portal platformers document "WASD or the Arrow Keys" for the same character; MDN's own game-controls tutorial teaches OR-checks between the two sets (PROVEN, `g4`, `g5`, `p5`).
- **Remapping is baseline, not a luxury — three independent authorities:** GAG lists "Allow controls to be remapped / reconfigured" at **Basic** tier ("easy to implement, wide reaching"); XAG 107 requires **in-game remapping of all controls "regardless of platform-level remapping support"**, ideally free assignment with prompts updating to match; Apple HIG's Designing for Games tells developers to let players customise "game control mapping" (PROVEN, `h3`, `h4`, `h1`; verifier-CONFIRMED tiers).
- **Prevalence:** GMTK's audit of 25 top AAA games found ~70% offer full remapping; GDC 2024 (>3,000 devs) put remapping as the #3 adopted accessibility measure at 48% (PROVEN for those samples; no source quantifies all shipped games — `p5`).
- **The browser removes the safety net — the strongest argument for this game.** Steam Input, Xbox Accessories, Switch and PS5 system remapping are all **controller-only, OS/client-level**, and Steam Input applies only to games launched through Steam. A bare Safari/Chrome tab — and iPhone entirely — has **no** platform remapping layer. A native game without remapping still leaves players a fallback; a web game without it leaves none (PROVEN, `p5`).
- **Browser-specific keyboard traps:** Space/arrows scroll the page unless the game calls `preventDefault()` (Poki's developer guide documents the exact fix, including a non-passive wheel listener); Escape must never be the sole pause key because the browser consumes it for fullscreen exit (PROVEN, `g5`, `p5`). WCAG 2.1.4 (character-key shortcuts) requires turn-off / remap-to-non-printable / active-only-on-focus for single-character shortcuts (PROVEN, `h5`).

### Q4 — Unified input models and iOS Safari pitfalls

- **Pointer Events are the unification layer, and Safari has supported them since Safari/iOS 13 (2019).** One abstract pointer covers mouse, pen, touch (incl. multi-touch); `pointerType` distinguishes them (trackpad reports as `mouse`); `buttons` is a bitmask for held-state; `setPointerCapture()` retargets a drag to one element; touch gets implicit capture, mouse does not — call it explicitly (PROVEN, W3C/MDN/caniuse, `t1`).
- **The framework world converges on an "action seam."** Phaser unifies mouse+touch into Pointer objects; Construct splits input by device object but reconverges through `Simulate control`; Godot's VirtualJoystick/TouchScreenButton and Defold's bindings all *write named actions* (`left`, `jump`, `interact`) that gameplay polls. That one architectural decision yields unification, remapping (a binding table), and virtual controls at once (PROVEN as framework design, `t4`).
- **`pointercancel` is the hold-killer.** The spec mandates it when the browser takes the pointer for panning/zooming, on orientation change, or palm rejection. On a game surface it is prevented by `touch-action: none` — which must be in effect *before* the gesture starts (PROVEN, `t1`, `t2`).
- **iOS Safari armour (each item PROVEN, `t2`):** viewport `maximum-scale`/`user-scalable=no` have been **ignored since iOS 10** — they give zero pinch protection; `-webkit-touch-callout: none` stops the hold callout; `-webkit-user-select: none` stops selection/magnifier; WebKit makes window/document `touchstart`/`touchmove` listeners **passive by default**, so `preventDefault()` in them silently does nothing unless `{passive: false}`; Safari's proprietary `gesturestart` must be `preventDefault()`ed or two-thumb play is read as a page pinch; the 300/350 ms tap delay is gone *if* the viewport is `width=device-width` at scale 1 — but games should still fire actions from `pointerdown`, not `click`; create/resume the AudioContext inside the first pointerdown.
- **Honest limit:** no primary source establishes how synthetic/injected touch behaves in Small Nukem's *test harness*, so "Pointer Events will fix the automated input-parity timeouts" is **UNPROVEN** — the cancel/`touch-action` findings explain real-device stuck holds; the harness question needs a separate empirical check (`t1` §5).

### Q5 — Trackpad

- **A trackpad is a mouse to the web platform, plus a wheel.** Tap-to-click, physical click, and drag all collapse into the standard pointer sequence (`pointerdown/move/up` + `click`); there is **no native hold/long-press event** for a mouse-type pointer — hold must be timer-synthesised from pointerdown/up with pointercancel handling (PROVEN, `t3`). So Kevin's directed trackpad parity (tap, drag, hold) arrives *free* through the unified pointer path; the only genuinely trackpad-specific surface is the wheel.
- **Wheel behaviour is where trackpads break games.** Two-finger scroll fires standard `wheel` events, but trackpads emit many small fractional deltas (1–10 px) with `deltaMode` varying by browser, plus an anonymous **momentum tail** after the fingers lift (macOS proves momentum natively; the web `WheelEvent` exposes no phase). Per-event or sign-only stepping explodes: a documented normaliser accumulates to a ~40 px notch threshold, and raw forwarding multiplied scroll ~40–100× in one shipped codebase (PROVEN, `t3`).
- **Pinch is split by browser:** Chrome/Firefox deliver trackpad pinch as `wheel` with `ctrlKey=true`; Safari uses its proprietary GestureEvent — handling only ctrl+wheel page-zooms in Safari (PROVEN, `t3`).
- **Shipped deliberate uses exist:** Phaser added wheel support explicitly for zoom/scroll in games; the Figma/Maps/tldraw pattern is ctrl+wheel = zoom, plain wheel = pan; a browser shooter (Poom) uses wheel to switch weapons (PROVEN, `t3`). Chrome makes root wheel listeners passive by default — `{passive: false}` is required to preventDefault (PROVEN, `t3`).
- **User settings compete:** macOS lets users disable two-finger scroll, remap gestures, and turn on swipe-between-pages, which fights horizontal in-game swipes (PROVEN, Apple support via `t3`).

### Q6 — Time-pressured precise control

- **Pause-to-plan is the operation-sim answer.** FTL markets "Play at Your Own Speed": pause mid-combat, issue the full command set (power, crew, targeting), execute on resume. On iPad it goes further — **auto-pause triggers on selecting** door controls, a crew member, or weapon targeting, which converts mis-taps from fatal to "mildly annoying" (PROVEN, `g7`).
- **Freeze-while-aiming exists in a platformer.** Celeste's Dash Assist freezes the game at the moment of the precise directional input; its Assist Mode also offers game speed down to 50% in 10% steps for "more reaction time" (PROVEN, `g4`, `p4`).
- **Slow-time is a shipped, input-tuned technique.** SUPERHOT's time-moves-when-you-move is its core mechanic, explicitly so the player can "assess their situation in slow motion"; Max Payne's console version used *deeper* bullet-time specifically "to make aiming easier on a control pad" — slowdown depth tuned per input device (PROVEN, `p4`). RimWorld auto-locks to speed 1 in combat; KSP2 allows pausing while interacting with controls for "last-second tweaks" (PROVEN, `p4`).
- **Forgiveness is a documented stack.** Celeste's developer documents coyote time (0.1 s in released code), jump buffering, corner correction — "everything is fudged a tiny bit in the player's favor." The transferable analogue for a lever is friction/snap near the target value, as Halo's aim magnetism/reticle friction does for aim (PROVEN techniques; transfer SUGGESTED, `p4`).
- **Deleting an input is also an assist.** Mario Kart 8 Deluxe's Auto-Accelerate removes the hold input entirely; Smart Steering bounds another. Transfer: once the first emergency control is set correctly, auto-hold it so the player fights one control at a time (PROVEN features; transfer SUGGESTED, `p4`).
- **The guidelines' floor:** GAG (Motor, **Advanced**): "Do not make precise timing essential to gameplay – offer alternatives, actions that can be carried out while paused, or a skip mechanism." XAG 107/108 offer the compliant shapes: sequence-speed setting, per-mechanic timing-window difficulty, less demanding alternative, checkpoint before, or skip. XAG 116 (time limits) governs *UI* limits only and explicitly excludes core gameplay — do not cite it either way on the emergency (PROVEN, `h3`, `h4`).
- **Honest gap:** no shipped game was found that enlarges critical controls during alarm states (searched, `p4` Q5). Only non-game emergency-UX guidance supports bigger targets under stress. Any "emergency mode enlarges the lever" idea is a design invention, not a researched pattern.

## 3. Recommendations for Small Nukem

### 3.1 Verdict on key remapping: **REMAP — both halves**

1. **Expand the defaults so both keyboard families are live at once** (arrows *and* WASD for movement/climb; jump/interact bound on both the Z/X/C cluster and Space/J/K side). Evidence: the two families are both "standard" and mutually exclusive in shipped defaults (`p5`); dual-binding is precedented in Celeste, Shovel Knight, and portal platformers (`g4`, `g5`, `p5`); MDN teaches the OR-check pattern (`g5`). This is a default-binding change, cheap, and helps every desktop player immediately.
2. **Add in-game remapping** — free assignment per action, saved (localStorage), on-screen prompts updating to the player's bindings, reset-to-default. Evidence: GAG **Basic** tier; XAG 107 requires it in-game *regardless of platform remapping*; Apple HIG Designing for Games recommends customisable control mapping; ~70% of audited top AAA games and 48% of GDC-surveyed devs ship it; and decisively, **a browser game has no platform fallback** — Steam Input doesn't reach a bare browser tab and iPhone has nothing (`h1`, `h3`, `h4`, `p5`). Kevin pre-approved remapping in principle; the research supports it without reservation.
   - One counter-note, honestly recorded: portal norms (Poki/CrazyGames) treat remapping as an encouraged extra, and one worker suggested skipping a remap UI in favour of dual-binding alone (`g5`). The guideline evidence (three authorities, one of them a *requirement*-strength "must provide in-game") outweighs portal custom for a game whose audience includes an operator-training context — but dual-binding is the higher-value half if sequencing forces a choice.

### 3.2 Architecture (the change everything else hangs on)

- **Route mouse, touch, and trackpad through ONE Pointer Events path** into a named-action layer (`left`, `right`, `up`, `down`, `interact`, `show`, …); gameplay polls action state per frame and reads edges from events. This is the convergent framework pattern (Phaser Pointer, Construct Simulate control, Godot/Defold action binding) and it is what makes remapping a binding-table edit instead of a rewrite (PROVEN pattern, `t1`, `t4`).
- **Track every active pointer by `pointerId`** (multi-touch: move + interact simultaneously), call `setPointerCapture()` explicitly for drags/holds, and **handle `pointercancel` as a first-class "release"** (PROVEN, `t1`, `p1`).
- **Ship the iOS Safari armour on the game surface:** `touch-action: none`, `-webkit-touch-callout: none`, `-webkit-user-select: none`, `preventDefault()` on `gesturestart`, non-passive listeners where preventDefault is needed, actions fired from `pointerdown` (not `click`), AudioContext resumed inside the first pointerdown (PROVEN, `t2`). Do not rely on viewport zoom-lock meta values — Safari has ignored them since iOS 10 (PROVEN, `t2`).
- **Keyboard:** `preventDefault()` on arrows/space in a keydown listener so the page never scrolls (Poki's documented fix); never make Escape the *sole* pause (PROVEN, `g5`, `p5`).

### 3.3 Touch hold-buttons (movement/climb)

- **Keep hold-to-move** — it is the shipped norm across every comparator (PROVEN, §Q1/Q2). Do not switch to tap-to-move as the primary scheme.
- **Replace separate per-direction buttons with ONE segmented movement zone**: a single touch surface whose left/right/up/down sectors are read from the active pointer's position, so a thumb can slide between directions without lifting, and a hold is one pointer's continuous contact — not four elements racing for touch events. Evidence: Carnage's slide-without-lifting fix and the independent web-devlog abandonment of separate `<button>` elements (PROVEN shipped fixes, `p1`); buttons-over-joystick accuracy study (PROVEN, `p1`). This is also the most plausible research-side explanation for live problem (b) — separate elements + injected holds + browser gesture takeover is exactly the failure shape the literature describes (SUGGESTED diagnosis; the ship #24 lane's A12/A13 code trace owns the actual verdict).
- **Size hit areas larger than the artwork**: ≥44 pt (Apple) / ≥48 dp (Material) as floors, ~15 mm (XAG phone suggestion) as the design target for the movement zone and interact; extend the active area beyond the drawn button (Carnage's "players thought the game was broken" bug) (PROVEN, `h1`, `h2`, `h4`, `p1`).
- **Offer a hold alternative**: a settings toggle — hold-to-move vs tap-to-toggle-move — per GAG Intermediate and XAG 107 (PROVEN requirement-strength guidance, `h3`, `h4`). Also allow button **position/size adjustment** (Dead Cells ships exactly this on mobile; Terraria ships a full editor with profiles) (PROVEN, `g1`, `g2`).
- **Interact stays one context button** (Limbo/Terraria/Dead Cells convergence, PROVEN, §Q1), placed bottom-right clear of the movement zone, ≥ the same size floors.

### 3.4 Panels and the dwell problem

- **Make a deliberate Interact press the primary way to open/operate a panel.** No comparator operates panels by presence alone; Limbo's closest precedent is stand-in-front + tap (PROVEN, `g8`). A press is unambiguous, testable (a discrete event, unlike a silent timer), and immune to the Midas Touch problem (PROVEN problem, `p2`).
- **If dwell is kept at all, rebuild it to the documented pattern**: onset delay ~200 ms, a **visible radial-fill progress indicator** from the moment the dwell starts, total duration **~0.8–1.5 s** (not 3 s — gaze norms are 0.4–1.0 s; XAG flags 2–3 s holds as exclusionary), a movement tolerance so small drifts don't reset it, and **pause-not-reset** behaviour (PROVEN pattern elements, `p2`; application SUGGESTED). A silent 3 s timer with reset-on-drift violates every documented norm and is the research-side explanation for why testers "couldn't tell a timer was running or why it reset" (SUGGESTED, `p2`).

### 3.5 Levers / valves — precise values per input method

The governing principle (NN/g + Apple HIG, PROVEN, `p3`): **a slider alone cannot do precision — every authority pairs a coarse control with a fine control.**

- **All inputs — two-stage commit** (Terraria's Aim Only → commit, PROVEN `g2`): adjusting a lever shows the pending value; a separate commit/apply action sets it. This separates "aim precisely" from "act", and under time pressure lets the player pre-stage a value.
- **Snap + readout:** detents/stops at operationally meaningful values with a snap (Material "stops" model; HIG tick marks "increase clarity and accuracy"), plus a **live numeric % readout** and a synced numeric field/stepper as the fine channel (NN/g: slider = coarse, numeric input + steppers = fine; Apple HIG says the same) (PROVEN, `h1`, `h2`, `p3`).
- **Touch:** drag = coarse with detents and haptic ticks on detent pass (iOS selection haptics are the platform mechanism, PROVEN `p3`); **stepper − / + buttons** flanking the lever for exact values; optionally the iOS fine-scrub gesture (drag perpendicular to slow the gearing — real and shipped in AVPlayer, gears to 0.1×) — but it is **not in the HIG and is undiscoverable**, so if used it must be labelled on-screen (PROVEN behaviour, documentation gap, `p3`).
- **Keyboard:** the one real standard — W3C ARIA APG slider pattern: **arrows = 1 step, Page Up/Down = larger step (10), Home/End = min/max** (PROVEN, `p3`). Modifier-for-fine is *not* standardised (Shift means ×10 on steppers but fine-drag in audio apps — pick one meaning, label it, don't do both) (PROVEN conflict, `p3`).
- **Mouse:** drag coarse; **wheel over a focused lever = fine adjust** with accumulated, normalised deltas (see trackpad); double-click = reset to a default/detent (the TT-303 manual ships the full stack: vertical drag, modifier-fine, wheel ≈2%/notch, double-click reset) (PROVEN, `p3`).
- **Hold-to-ramp:** where a hold adjusts a value, use the iOS `UIStepper` autorepeat shape — tap = 1 step, hold = accelerating ramp (PROVEN platform behaviour, `p3`) — flanked by **instant end-stops and presets** as the flight-sim world does: KSP Z = full / X = cut, Elite Dangerous bindable 0/25/50/75/100% set-points and a throttle-increments setting (PROVEN, `p3`). For a plant game, preset keys/buttons for standard set-points (e.g., the exact bypass/lever values procedures call for) are the single highest-leverage precision feature in this section (SUGGESTED ranking, evidence PROVEN).
- **Stardew's warning, applied:** an accelerating hold-counter is a documented overshoot machine for exact numbers — never make hold-to-count the *only* way to reach a precise value (PROVEN failure mode, `g3`).

### 3.6 Trackpad mapping

- **Tap, drag, hold: nothing special to build** — they arrive through the pointer path as mouse-type events; hold is timer-synthesised from pointerdown/up with pointercancel handled (PROVEN, `t3`). Kevin's "full trackpad parity" is satisfied by the unified architecture in §3.2, not by a trackpad code path.
- **Swipe (two-finger scroll) → value adjustment on the hovered/focused lever**, implemented as: listen to `wheel` with `{ passive: false }`, normalise by `deltaMode`, **accumulate deltas to a notch threshold (~40 px of accumulated delta = one step)** instead of stepping per event, and **clamp/damp the momentum tail** so the value stops when the gesture stops (PROVEN failure modes and fixes, `t3`). Treat `ctrlKey` wheel (pinch) as zoom only, and in Safari also `preventDefault()` the proprietary gesture events (PROVEN, `t3`).
- **Do not** map raw wheel deltas 1:1 to value changes (documented 40–100× blow-up), and do not use sign-only stepping (documented trackpad explosion) (PROVEN, `t3`).

### 3.7 The Level 3 emergency (~12 s, two precise changes)

Ranked by strength of shipped precedent — the *choice among these is a game-design decision for Kevin*, because each changes how the emergency feels; the research establishes that the current shape (two precise real-time drags, no assistance) is the one shape no comparator ships unassisted:

1. **Freeze-while-adjusting (FTL auto-pause pattern):** engaging the emergency lever/valve pauses or freezes the sim clock while the value is set; releasing commits and resumes. FTL's iPad auto-pause on control selection is the direct precedent; Celeste's Dash Assist freeze-while-aiming is the platformer precedent (PROVEN, `g7`, `g4`).
2. **Presets for the emergency values** (§3.5): if the procedure calls for bypass ≥97 and lever ≤13, one tap/key should be able to *set* those values exactly — Elite/KSP-style set-points (PROVEN pattern, `p3`). Combined with two-stage commit, the emergency becomes "stage both, commit" — FTL's queue-while-paused in miniature (SUGGESTED combination).
3. **A game-speed / sequence-speed option** (Celeste Assist 50–100%; XAG 108 difficulty shapes) for players who need the window wider (PROVEN, `g4`, `h4`).
4. **Snap/friction near target values** during the emergency (forgiveness stack, §Q6) (PROVEN technique, transfer SUGGESTED, `p4`).
- What the research does **not** support: enlarging the controls during the alarm (no shipped precedent found) — if the design wants it, it's an invention, not a pattern (PROVEN absence, `p4`).

## 4. For the ship #24 lane — change vs leave alone

### Change (research-backed)
1. **Input plumbing:** one Pointer Events path + named-action layer; per-pointerId tracking; explicit pointer capture; pointercancel handled as release; iOS armour (`touch-action: none`, callout/user-select off, gesturestart guard, non-passive listeners, pointerdown-fired actions). (§3.2 — feeds their A12 hold-button trace: check the current code against the pointercancel/touch-action findings first.)
2. **Touch movement:** separate hold-buttons → one segmented movement zone with slide-between-directions; hit areas larger than artwork, ≥44 pt floor / ~15 mm target. (§3.3)
3. **Panel opening:** Interact press becomes the primary open/operate path. (§3.4 — this also gives their dwell defect a designed alternative whatever the code trace finds.)
4. **Dwell, if retained:** visible radial progress, ~0.8–1.5 s, onset delay, movement tolerance, pause-not-reset. Never silent, never 3 s, never the only path. (§3.4)
5. **Lever/valve control:** detents + snap, live numeric readout, stepper fine channel, two-stage commit; keyboard APG pattern (arrows 1 step, PgUp/PgDn 10, Home/End). (§3.5)
6. **Keyboard defaults:** both families live simultaneously; preventDefault on arrows/space; Escape not sole pause. (§3.1, §3.2)
7. **Trackpad:** wheel-over-lever fine adjust with deltaMode normalisation, ~40 px accumulation threshold, momentum clamp; `{passive:false}`. (§3.6)
8. **Emergency assist (needs Kevin's design pick from §3.7):** freeze-while-adjusting and/or emergency presets. Flagged, not pre-decided.

### Build next (approved in principle by Kevin; sequence after #24 if the ship is full)
9. **In-game remapping UI** — free assignment, saved bindings, prompts follow bindings, reset-to-default. (§3.1)

### Leave alone
- **Hold-to-move as the basic touch model** — it is the industry norm; the defect is the button implementation, not the hold. (§Q2)
- **The single context-sensitive Interact verb** — convergent shipped design; don't add per-panel buttons. (§Q1)
- **Samira's model/physics numbers** — untouched by everything here; controls research gives no reason to change any value in the model.
- **The mouse path as a separate scheme** — mouse needs no dedicated design once the pointer path is unified; inventing one would add a second code path for nothing. (§Q4)
- **Tap-to-move** — do not add it as a primary scheme (Stardew's own wiki documents its precision ceiling); the toggle alternative in §3.3 is an accessibility option, not a second primary. (§Q1, §Q2)
